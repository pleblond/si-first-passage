## Main constructive contributions in this draft

The shared-field SI estimator is symmetric and metric at every sample size, without coordinate access or a projection/repair step. Its population limit is the mean symmetric SI first-passage metric under the stated transmission law.

The finite-graph weak-shortcut theorem assumes an intact connected backbone, fixed baseline interactions, and finitely many added probabilities tending to zero. It proves uniform pairwise convergence to the backbone mean metric. It does not apply directly to swap damage with missing reference edges, a growing number of weak channels, or an unknown non-Euclidean baseline.

The Gram perturbation bound makes the distance-to-MDS implication explicit. Recovery of an MDS baseline follows when that baseline has suitable spectral separation. It does not establish the geometric approximation premise, and it does not claim novelty over the general perturbation literature.
