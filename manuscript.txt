Abstract
Binary shortcut metrics can obscure an underlying spatial geometry, but their failure is
not a universal collapse to rank two. We separate distance contraction, shell counting, loss
of low-dimensional spectral concentration, and finite-sample reconstruction error. Exact coun-
terexamples show that complete-graph hop metrics have rank N −1, while rescaled binary
paths converge to a continuous interval. The useful necessity statement is therefore conditional:
continuously adjustable coupling on a fixed graph requires state beyond binary adjacency, not a
rejection of every unweighted continuum limit.
We formulate a reference-based weighted audit and construct an SI first-passage estimator by
averaging shortest-path metrics over shared random edge fields. Every finite-sample estimate is
a metric; independently sampled source rows, even after symmetrization, need not be. We prove
recovery of the intact-backbone mean metric when a finite set of added shortcut probabilities
tends to zero, and give an explicit perturbation bound connecting metric recovery to classical
multidimensional scaling (MDS).
New computations improve the two-dimensional positive-spectral-mass score of a 10 × 10
grid from 0.7749 to 0.9151, with sample standard deviation 0.0005 across five Monte Carlo
replications. A three-dimensional control also improves. However, uniform SI does not remove
the radial mode of finite-box Delaunay graphs. A coordinate-length oracle sharply reduces that
distortion but is not an intrinsic reconstruction rule. Weighted coarsening, D11 tail diagnostics,
and disk-interface curvature are consequently assigned their precise, distinct evidential roles
rather than treated as a completed proof of universal manifold reconstruction.
Keywords: weighted graph metrics; susceptible–infectious dynamics; first-passage percolation;
multidimensional scaling; metric audits; geometric reconstruction.
1. Introduction
A graph does not determine a unique operational notion of distance. Hop count treats all present
edges as equally traversable; a weighted shortest-path metric assigns heterogeneous traversal costs;
an infection process measures arrival times generated by repeated interactions. These constructions

may produce different volume profiles and different MDS spectra on the same adjacency structure.
A reconstruction claim must therefore identify the metric being reconstructed, the variables carrying
its information, and the reference against which its error is assessed.
The preceding paper, Distance, Volume, and Apparent Dimension in Weighted Graphs, separated
pointwise metric contraction, ball-volume inflation, and changes in fitted growth slopes [1]. This
companion study adds a spectral reconstruction layer and a stochastic operational metric. Its
starting point is the research program recorded in the cursor/d1-dynamics-u-2031 branch of
alltoall-black-holes [2]. The branch’s labels D10, D10b, D11, and Tier-1 are retained to make
the correspondence explicit; they are project identifiers, not mathematical dimensions or theorem
numbers.
Three distinctions control the argument. First, a failure to recover a two-dimensional reference is not
evidence that a graph has collapsed into a two-dimensional state. In the grid controls, two dominant
MDS modes are a desired null signature. Second, a continuous metric can arise as a rescaled limit of
binary graphs. The motivation for weighted microscopic state is the ability to express heterogeneous
or continuously tunable interactions on a fixed topology, not an impossibility theorem for all binary
constructions. Third, SI first passage belongs to established stochastic growth and first-passage
percolation, rather than constituting a new general propagation mechanism [3, 4]. The contribution
here is its integration with metric-preserving estimation, explicit recovery conditions, and an audit
that detects misleading geometric conclusions.
Classical scaling reconstructs a Euclidean configuration from squared distances when its centered
Gram matrix is positive semidefinite [5]. Graph-based manifold learning likewise requires assumptions
connecting graph distances to a relevant continuum metric; the graph-to-geodesic step is not justified
by the final embedding alone [6]. Perturbation theory makes this separation quantitative: a good
embedding follows from controlled distance errors and suitable spectral conditioning, not merely
from choosing an output dimension [7].
The paper has three parts. Part I supplies exact diagnostics and counterexamples for binary metrics.
Part II states the weighted audit, its prospective registration requirements, and what a finite
coarsening pilot actually demonstrates. Part III gives the SI construction and recovery theorem,
followed by finite-dimensional calibration and deliberately retained negative controls. All newly
tabulated simulations were executed with the accompanying independent implementation. Historical
D11 observations are clearly marked as source-reported rather than newly reproduced.

5. What the weighted L0 claim can legitimately establish
Proposition 5.1 (An unweighted continuum counterexample). Let Pn be the binary path on vertices
0, . . . , n and equip it with the rescaled hop metric dn(i, j) = |i −j|/n. The map i 7→i/n is an
isometry into [0, 1]. Its image has Hausdorff distance at most 1/(2n) from the interval. Thus binary
paths, after a common change of units, approximate a continuous spatial metric arbitrarily well.
Proof. The unique path from i to j has |i −j| edges. Every point of the interval is within 1/(2n) of
a grid vertex, including the endpoints. Both conclusions follow directly.
Likewise, a uniformly rescaled nearest-neighbor lattice approximates the continuous ℓ1 metric

on a box of any fixed dimension.
This metric is not Euclidean, but it is continuous and its
underlying topology is the usual box topology. Therefore the universal statement that unweighted
or combinatorial models cannot support continuous spatial metrics is false. Even the binary-path
example already defeats it.
A narrower statement is useful. For fixed finite V , there are only finitely many simple binary
adjacency matrices.
A deterministic adjacency-only rule consequently has only finitely many
outputs. It cannot encode a nonconstant continuously varying family of coupling states while the
topology remains fixed. A weighted state G = (V, E, g) supplies such a parameter space, but it is
not the only logically possible extension: interaction-time distributions, additional vertex variables,
or distributions over graphs also carry nonbinary information.
The operational advantage of weighted L0 state is therefore flexibility and testability. A present
edge can become weak without being deleted, and its effect can be audited as its microscopic
interaction parameter changes. What remains to be derived in a physical application is why a
particular local state determines a particular cost or propagation law. That derivation cannot be
replaced by selecting weights that yield a preferred MDS dimension.

7. Weight selection and prospective pre-registration
A reconstruction audit cannot establish that a weight law was selected independently of the desired
reconstruction. That independence is a design requirement. Before a confirmatory run, a registration
should fix the allowed microscopic variables, the cost/probability map, all tunable parameters, graph
generators, random seeds or seed policy, reference metrics, calibration windows, boundary treatment,
and failure criteria.
A general local candidate might have the form
puv = min{1, p⋆fθ(χu, χv, Nuv)},
fθ > 0,
(15)
where Nuv is a specified local graph neighborhood and χ is an independently defined microscopic
variable. Equation (15) is a protocol template, not a derived law or a rule validated here. Its
parameters cannot be retuned on the evaluation graphs to maximize G2, remove a third mode, or
match target coordinates. Any conservation law Q, update operator U, and map from state to
interaction probability must be stated separately; conservation of a sum of numerical edge weights
is not assumed merely because the weights exist.
The primary computations in this draft use a frozen execution configuration, code/design.json,
written before their execution. The probability p = 1/2 is transferred without optimization. A
secondary configuration, extension_design.json, was specified after the initial batch and before
the dimension-control and Delaunay-SI transfer runs. Those secondary results are diagnostic, not a
previously registered confirmatory test. Neither file was deposited in an external, time-stamped
registration service. A hash fixes file contents; it does not prove an earlier public commitment.
Two forms of privileged information are explicitly retained as controls. The weak-shortcut experiment
knows which edges were added by its generator, and the Euclidean-length oracle knows the evaluation
coordinates. These controls test mathematical mechanisms and attainable reconstruction quality.
Neither is offered as a method for discovering unknown physical edge weights from adjacency alone.

9. A dynamical metric with a metric-preserving estimator
9.1. Persistent SI and random edge passage times

infected vertex attempts to infect each susceptible neighbor independently with probability pe ∈(0, 1].

transmitter after a failed attempt. The source branch implements this repeated-attempt convention

17. Discussion and conclusion
The binary/weighted distinction is not a dichotomy between impossible and inevitable geometry.
Binary adjacency can approximate a continuum under a specified scaling, but it cannot by itself
express continuously changing interactions on an unchanged finite graph. Dense unit-cost shortcuts
can erase reference spatial contrast, yet the resulting MDS failure need not be rank two and need
not occur after every added shortcut. Complete-graph equidistance and the new one-swap control
make those limitations exact and concrete.
The constructive result is a conditional recovery framework. Shared random edge fields yield
finite-sample SI metrics without symmetrization repairs. On an intact finite backbone, weakening
finitely many added interactions recovers the backbone’s mean metric, and controlled metric errors
imply controlled centered Gram matrices. The numerical weak-shortcut experiment illustrates that
mechanism. Independent one-, two-, and three-dimensional fixtures test reconstruction against
known geometry rather than claiming to derive dimension from the desired output.
Three boundaries remain decisive. Deterministic mean edge costs do not certify mean first-passage
distances. A static minimum-cost coarsening rule is not a closed SI renormalization transformation.
A finite spectral improvement is not a proof of global Euclidean geometry, boundary uniformity,
or manifold convergence. Even a correct intrinsic metric on a curved manifold need not have a
globally rank-d Euclidean MDS realization; intrinsic dimension and global embedding dimension are
different quantities.
D11 and D10b fit within this framework without being conflated. D11 records weighted finite-window
tail behavior whose interpretation depends on volume-ratio recovery and a separately specified
propagation law. D10b identifies an inclusion-interface curvature signature, not the absence of
boundaries. The Delaunay transfer test then supplies a direct falsifier of the strongest universal
reconstruction claim: uniform SI improves several measures but leaves a radial artifact, while a
coordinate oracle demonstrates a substantially better attainable control.
The resulting research program has a precise next acceptance condition: derive or independently
select an intrinsic local interaction rule, keep it fixed across substrate families and domain shapes,
and show that its metric, spectral, coordinate, and boundary diagnostics all meet declared tolerances.
The theorems proved here identify what follows once the relevant metric control is obtained. They
do not replace that empirical and analytical task with an assumed ontology or a tuned embedding.
